## 最終報告（Codex）

**The reduced design is suitable for an attended student build, with changes to STOP, VM sensing, and the interpretation of the current limit.** I would omit the external watchdog circuitry, keep the unbuffered mux for diagnostics, and prototype one DRV8874 channel before committing the PCB.

**[OBS]** = sourced observation; shop information checked **2026-10-15**. **[INF]** = calculation, recommendation, or budget estimate. Currency conversion: **US$1 = ¥150**.

**1. Verdicts on items 1–10**

| Item | Verdict | Required change or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **1. DRV8874 / current limit** | **Keep, with a more conservative initial setting.** | [INF] The proposed network gives **0.595 A nominal**, not a guaranteed 0.6 A ceiling. Initially populate **5.1 kΩ instead of 6.8 kΩ**, giving approximately **0.496 A nominal**; retain the option to increase after testing. Keep PMODE high, IMODE floating, and 4.99 kΩ IPROPI resistors. |
| **2. Internal watchdog / STOP** | **Omitting the external watchdog is reasonable; change STOP.** | [INF] Use a **maintained STOP/RUN switch**, preferably an NC contact in series with ARM, with the pull-down on the driver side. The proposed momentary grounding button stops the bridges while held, but releasing it can immediately restart them. Details below. |
| **3. GPIO allocation** | **Fits.** | [INF] All **26 exposed GPIOs are used**. GPIO-mode aliases do not interfere with PWM. Disable UART0 TX explicitly before initializing Serial1, or GP0 conflicts with DIP5. Restrict independent-channel mode to base channels **1–13**. |

The current filter modifies the instantaneous regulation threshold and has leakage-related errors; it is unsuitable for calibrated RMS current or peak protection. The driver’s current-mirror tolerance and regulation timing reinforce the need to distinguish a nominal trip setting from an absolute peak ceiling. IMODE floating selects fixed-off-time regulation with latched OCP response. [OBS: **DRV8874 datasheet**, §6.5 and §7.3.3, pp. 6, 13–14](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/DRV8874)

**Watchdog and STOP implementation**

[INF] Recommended connection:

```text
GP14 ARM ── 1 kΩ ── maintained NC STOP contact ── common nSLEEP
                                                   │
                                                 10 kΩ
                                                   │
                                                  GND
```

Keep external **10 kΩ pull-downs on all eight IN pins**.

The internal-watchdog approach covers ordinary CPU hangs, stalled audio production, and link loss. With the proposed feed condition, the nominal worst case is approximately **350 + 100 + 1 = 451 ms**, including driver sleep entry.

Make these firmware changes:

- Use the SDK’s full-reset watchdog configuration, with **pause-on-debug disabled during powered motor testing**.
- When the lease expires, **lower ARM immediately**; do not deliberately wait for the watchdog.
- Continue feeding a healthy main loop while safely disarmed. Otherwise, an absent bus causes an unnecessary reset loop.
- Check **producer sequence/freshness**, not merely DMA completion interrupts: DMA can repeatedly play stale data.
- Latch driver/temperature faults in software; require deliberate recovery. Never resume a pre-reset note from retained state.

[OBS] The SDK resets the chip except its oscillators, and the watchdog tick derives from `clk_ref`. An external watchdog adds protection against a stopped watchdog clock or accidentally disabled/misconfigured watchdog. It does **not** solve faulty health logic that continues generating valid kicks. [SDK watchdog implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raspberrypi/pico-sdk/master/src/rp2_common/hardware_watchdog/watchdog.c), [RP2040 datasheet, §4.7](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)

[INF] **For this attended contest project, I would spend the effort on fault-injection tests rather than restore all three ICs.** If the 500 ms requirement must cover oscillator/watchdog failures too, retain an independent watchdog. A maintained STOP also avoids depending on software to detect a button press: reading GP14 upstream of the 1 kΩ does not reliably reveal that the original downstream STOP button is grounding nSLEEP.

**Mux and sensing details**

[INF] The proposed filter is usable, but its error is larger than “a few insignificant microamps” suggests:

- Initially, the 100 nF capacitor makes the extra branch approximately a **100 kΩ load**. Together with 4.99 kΩ, this reduces IPROPI voltage by **4.75%**, potentially raising the trip current by about **5%**.
- Effective filter time constant is approximately **10.5 ms**, or **15 Hz** bandwidth.
- **1 µA leakage through 100 kΩ creates 100 mV error**, equivalent to approximately **45 mA** using nominal IPROPI scaling.
- The capacitor supports ADC acquisition, but cannot eliminate steady leakage error.

[OBS] SN74LV4052A specifies switch leakage up to 0.1 µA at 25 °C and 1 µA over −40–85 °C under its stated test conditions. RP2040 documents a capacitive ADC input and separately specifies GPIO leakage. [Mux datasheet, §5.5](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lv4052a.pdf), [RP2040 datasheet, §4.9.2 and §5.5.3](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)

[INF] Start with 50–100 current reports/s/channel and 10 temperature reports/s/channel. After changing mux selection, wait around 10 µs, discard the first conversion, and average a small burst. Validate against a meter/current probe. Keep INH grounded, both mux ground pins grounded, and supply it from 3V3.

**Filtered IPROPI is neither signed current nor RMS current.** Its observability depends on the bridge state. Treat it as a diagnostic trend; use the probe for characterization. [OBS: **DRV8874**, §7.3.3.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/DRV8874)

[INF] NTC open circuit and short circuit must both produce a fault. A sensor “near the coil” is **not thermal truth**: its lag and temperature difference from the winding need measurement before accepting 55/60 °C thresholds.

**VM sensing correction**

[INF] At 15 V, assuming a 0.3 V clamp into an unpowered pin, the proposed divider injects about **25 µA**, rather than a 20 µA worst case. Small current alone does not establish a documented operating condition.

Use BSS84 source at VM, drain feeding the divider; gate pulled to source, and pulled down through an NPN when 3V3 exists. Retain a gate zener and current-limiting resistor. This needs **no GPIO**. RP2040 explicitly warns that ADC voltages above IOVDD cause ESD-diode leakage. [OBS: **RP2040**, §4.9](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)

**Concrete minimal pickup circuit**

[INF] Use **MCP6004-I/P + MCP6022-I/P**, powered from 5 V:

```text
Each piezo ── 47 kΩ ── high-Z follower ── 10 kΩ level pot ── 47 kΩ ─┐
               │                                                 │
        2.2 MΩ to VMID                                           ├─ inverting sum
        low-leakage rail clamps                                  │   referenced to VMID
        optional 100 pF to VMID                         four inputs┘
                                                                    │
                                                         10 µF ── 100 Ω ── line out

Protected 9–15 V ── 22 Ω / 100 µF filter ── 78L05 ── analog 5 V
Analog 5 V ── 10 kΩ / 10 kΩ + 47 µF ── follower ── VMID ≈ 2.5 V
```

- Four MCP6004 sections are unity-gain input buffers.
- MCP6022 section A buffers VMID; section B sums.
- **Pot bottoms connect to VMID**, not ground.
- Start with summing feedback **47 kΩ**, giving unity gain per fully opened channel. Provide a **220 kΩ option** for weak pickups; set trims to prevent summed clipping. A 22 pF feedback capacitor is a reasonable starting value.
- Use low-leakage clamps such as BAV199 to the 0/5 V rails after the input resistor. Keep high-impedance nodes short and clean.
- Add local 100 nF bypasses, regulator capacitors, and an output discharge resistor. Output electrolytic positive terminal faces the op-amp.
- Use 3.5 mm inputs wired as mono and a **6.3 mm TS unbalanced line output**. This is for a PA/mixer input, not headphones.

[OBS] MCP6022 is unity-gain stable, supports 2.5–5.5 V supplies, and has low input bias current. MCP6004’s lower bandwidth is still sufficient for these unity-gain audio buffers. [MCP6022 datasheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/DeviceDoc/20001685E.pdf), [Microchip MCP600x datasheet hosted by Akizuki](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/MCP6001_1R_1U_2_4.pdf), [OPA4134 common-mode limits](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa4134.pdf)

[INF] A borrowed mixer is preferable **if it actually has four suitable high-impedance inputs**. Four ordinary line inputs do not replace these buffers. One DI does not mix four pickups; four active piezo DIs are considerably more expensive. A single DI after this board remains useful for a long PA cable.

**B. DRV8874 at 62.5 kHz**

[OBS] The driver accepts up to 100 kHz PWM. Its listed typical timings include approximately **400 ns propagation delay, 100 ns dead time and 150 ns rise/fall times**; the datasheet does not establish an 8 ns minimum recognized pulse. Wake time is specified as 1 ms. [DRV8874, §6.3–6.5](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/DRV8874)

| Issue | Design implication [INF] |
|---|---|
| **2000 timer counts** | Approximately 10.97-bit *command* resolution. This does not establish 11-bit analog linearity. |
| **Propagation delay** | A constant common delay shifts the waveform; it does not by itself quantize duty into 400 ns steps. Delay mismatch and jitter matter. |
| **Locked-antiphase** | Quiet audio uses pulses near 8 µs, avoiding narrow-pulse problems. Dead-time voltage error changes with current direction and can cause crossover distortion. |
| **Drive/brake sign-magnitude** | Lower idle switching and better IPROPI observability, but a 1% command produces only a **160 ns drive pulse**. Low-level distortion needs measurement. |
| **Dead-time scale** | A simple estimate, \(2V_Mt_d/T\), gives about **0.15 V at 12 V**. This is an error scale, not a guaranteed distortion specification. |
| **Current limiting** | Keep ordinary audio below the chopping threshold. Chopping is protection and will alter the waveform. |

[INF] **Use drive/brake as the initial conservative default**, with locked-antiphase available for the A/B test. Switch the performance default if locked-antiphase gives materially cleaner low-level sound at acceptable heating.

For locked-antiphase, the low-inductance case can have roughly **46 mA RMS carrier current at zero command**, using 12 V and 0.6 mH in a simple inductive approximation. Therefore, use **00 coast between notes**, rather than continuously transmitting 50% duty.

[OBS] In PWM mode, **00 = coast**, **11 = low-side brake**. Coast leaves the device awake; sleep powers down internal circuitry. [DRV8874, §7.3.2 and §7.4](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/DRV8874)

[INF] Keep drivers awake between nearby notes. After raising nSLEEP, initially allow **2 ms** before playback and verify startup experimentally. **A locked-antiphase duty of zero is full drive in one direction—not OFF.** On shutdown, lower ARM first, then stop DMA/PWM and force both inputs low.

**5. Remaining risks and tests before ordering**

- **Current-limit overshoot:** measure both polarities, low-inductance loads, startup, waveform saturation and transitions into chopping. Neither the original nor reduced reference network guarantees an absolute 0.6 A peak.
- **Thermal lag:** compare NTC readings with winding heating during the worst sustained waveform. Validate the sensor attachment; a detached sensor can report a plausible but misleading temperature.
- **Fail-safe timing:** deliberately stall core0, stall core1, freeze buffer production while DMA continues, disable interrupts, and disconnect the bus. Measure actual output shutdown. Test STOP while firmware is stalled.
- **PWM fidelity:** compare low-level sine waves and note attacks in both modes using a current probe and recorded pickup output. Establish a usable minimum pulse width experimentally.
- **Power sequencing:** test USB-only, VM-only, simultaneous power, startup/inrush, four-channel stop/regeneration and brownout recovery. Verify that no stale note resumes.
- **Pickup and PCB noise:** establish pickup position and gain on the real HDD. Keep piezo wiring away from bridge outputs and the buck. Use a continuous ground plane, local bridge-current loops, Kelvin sense returns and properly soldered thermal pads.

**The minimum useful pre-PCB prototype is one reconfigured #4035, one actual HDD, its attached NTC, and one pickup buffer.** That resolves the largest uncertainties without purchasing four carriers first.
