## Design in one paragraph (for orientation; the files are authoritative)
Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) on a 155x115 mm single-sided perfboard, 4x Pololu #4035 DRV8874 carriers (R2 and R4 removed, R3=2.49 k kept, PMODE tied to 3.3 V, IMODE floating), one shared VREF divider 3.3 V-47k-VREF-10k-GND + 220 nF for all four (I_TRIP ~0.52 A nominal), common SLEEP from GP14 via 1 k and a maintained STOP toggle with 10 k pull-down, FAULTs wired-OR to GP15 with 10 k pull-up, no external IN pull-downs (relying on DRV8874 internal ~100 k and RP2040 reset pull-downs), 12 V 3 A adapter -> MF-R300 polyfuse -> 1N5822 series diode -> power toggle -> VM, P4KE15A TVS, 470 uF per carrier + 470 uF at entry, M78AR05-1 (5 V/1 A) -> 1N5819 -> Pico VSYS, SmartNet RX via 1 k series + 47 k pull-up (no Ioff buffer), synthesized audio PWM 62.5 kHz into IN1/IN2 (drive/brake default), internal RP2040 watchdog 100 ms + 350 ms link lease, no temperature/current/voltage sensing (thermal handled by firmware limits calibrated in a prototype test), VCM coil wires via pre-crimped JST VH leads, HDDs screwed to MDF/plywood panels, optional separate piezo pickup board.

**SmartNet compliance is achievable, but not demonstrated.** [INF] The proposed conservative budget of 350 + 100 + 1 = 451 ms fits 500 ms, leaving approximately 49 ms for unaccounted scheduling/reception effects. Measure actual output/current shutdown, not merely an LED or software timestamp.

The essential firmware details are:

- **Startup:** establish GP14 LOW and IN = 00 before enabling output functions. Let VREF settle; establish PMODE before enabling SLEEP; allow wake time before starting PWM. Initialize the watchdog before permitting drive.
- **Shutdown:** lower SLEEP before any potentially blocking DMA cleanup. For CH_OFF, force that channel’s two physical pins LOW even while other channels continue.
- **Truth table:** persistent **10 or 01** means directional drive; **11** means brake; **00** means coast. “100% PWM” is ambiguous because the drive/brake mapping may invert a GPIO’s duty. Test the actual pin pairs.
- **Freshness:** check both generated-buffer sequence and DMA consumption. A reasonable initial engineering target is to detect missing approximately 2 ms buffers within a few milliseconds, rather than waiting 100 ms.
- **Watchdog:** feed only after the complete safety check. Once safely stopped, stale audio buffers need not cause endless watchdog resets. Disable debug-pause behavior for powered fault tests.
- **CRC/STATE:** isolated errors can recover through STATE. Persistent errors/overflow need visible diagnostics and a defined stop policy. Heartbeats can remain valid while note/state traffic is damaged. If adding STATE-age limits, account for SmartNet sending **one channel’s STATE per 50 ms**, not every channel every 50 ms.
- **Ownership/restart:** verify actual SmartSlave callback behavior for timeout, ALL_OFF and CH_OFF; explicitly register GP14 as a safe pin. A fault reset followed by an old STATE must not unexpectedly restart the instrument.

**3. Top five changes before buying everything / first power-on**

1. **Keep the one-channel qualification gate and lower the initial VREF ceiling.** Verify each modified carrier on a dummy load before connecting a coil. Raise the ceiling only from measured acoustic, current and thermal results.
2. **Make shutdown and rearming explicit.** Add STOP/SLEEP readback, latch faults, clear playback state, and implement watchdog/DMA checks before developing elaborate sound synthesis.
3. **Restore the small UART receiver protection if accidental adapter loss must be harmless.** Approximately ¥130 of additional purchases plus an existing capacitor is a proportionate exception to the simplification. If omitted, document the residual risk and require physical bus disconnection during power changes.
4. **Finish a real wiring and mechanical drawing.** Resolve VH mounting, DC-jack support, screw lengths, stationary-flex terminations, supply returns, module retention and guards before committing to the full build.
5. **Turn S8–S10 into pass/fail acceptance tests for the completed instrument.** Include all four actual HDDs, warm repeated performances, USB/VM transitions, STOP release, individual FAULT paths, stale DMA, full-drive dummy-load faults, and transport followed by retesting.

**4. Contest-day checklist**

- **Transport:** protect arms and platters with a cover; carry by the base; restrain cables and socketed modules. Mark mounting screws so loosening is visible. Keep each HDD matched to its calibrated channel.
- **Before power:** inspect connectors, solder joints, guards and arm clearance. Confirm the labelled adapter is the qualified 12 V unit. STOP engaged; SmartNet disconnected.
- **Setup:** power the instrument and master independently, confirm the instrument remains disarmed, then connect SmartNet and send ALL_OFF. Use the power-up sequence that passed the inrush test.
