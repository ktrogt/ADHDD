## Requirements from Claude (design to these)
- 4 VCM channels, full H-bridge each. Load: coil R 5-40 ohm, L 0.6-4 mH (measured ranges from literature; actual junk drives e.g. Seagate ST1000DM003 unknown). Typical current 0.1-0.5 A, short peaks up to ~1 A. VM 12 V nominal (allow 9-15 V; a 24 V option is nice-to-have, not required).
- Two drive styles must both be possible from the same hardware:
  (a) note-frequency bipolar square wave 30 Hz-2 kHz (PIO or PWM), with a hardware current limit;
  (b) synthesized waveform: PWM carrier 40-62.5 kHz with ~10-11 bit duty resolution, duty updated at audio rate via DMA (locked-antiphase or sign-magnitude). The driver must accept this input frequency and must not distort it badly.
- Adjustable hardware current limit per channel (protection / volume) that is safe at reset.
- Per-channel current sensing readable by the Pico is desirable (calibration, thermal estimation, fault detection). Pico exposes only 3 ADC pins (GP26-28) -> evaluate analog mux vs external SPI/I2C ADC vs driver features (e.g. IPROPI current mirror).
- Optional per-HDD coil temperature sensing (NTC glued near the coil, on a connector) - evaluate cost/benefit.
- Fault reporting from drivers (nFAULT etc.) to the Pico.
- Fail-safe: driver inputs/sleep pulled to OFF at boot/reset/Pico unpowered; a way to hard-disable all drivers (e.g. common nSLEEP/EN line with pull-down, driven by Pico; consider an independent hardware watchdog/deadman if cheap).
- UART RX from the SmartNet bus (3.3 V TTL, 38400 bps): input buffer with Ioff (e.g. 74LVC1G17) + series R + pull-up; JST XH 2P. Optional pass-through connector for daisy-chaining the bus.
- Power: 12 V input via DC jack 5.5/2.1 and a screw terminal; reverse-polarity protection (P-MOSFET preferred over diode), fuse or polyfuse, TVS, bulk capacitance; 5 V buck for Pico VSYS through a Schottky (Pico datasheet recommendation) so USB can be connected at the same time; VM voltage monitor to an ADC pin.
- UI: SmartNet channel selection (DIP switch or rotary hex switch - mind GPIO count), per-channel activity LEDs, power LED, accessible Pico BOOTSEL/RESET (RUN) button, test points (VM, 5 V, 3V3, GND, each channel's current sense), mounting holes.
- Connectors: coil connectors must be mechanically incompatible with the UART connector (keyed, different size). Consider JST VH or XH 3P center-empty, or screw terminals.
- Optional: header for future expansion (e.g. 2 more channels, piezo pickup preamp, NeoPixel LEDs).

## 1. Recommendation summary

All recommendations below are **[INF]**.

- Use **4 × DRV8874PWPR**, HTSSOP-16 with exposed pad. Prototype its PWM behavior first.
- Retain **DRV8876PWPR** as an assembly variant, changing its current-sense resistor and calibration. It has better low-current sensing accuracy, but less favorable published switching behavior.
- Configure **PWM input mode**, allowing either complementary locked-antiphase or sign-magnitude drive/brake operation.
- Give each bridge its own **PWM-filtered VREF**, with a resistor-defined maximum: initially approximately **0.6 A**, optionally approximately **1 A** after coil testing.
- Read four buffered IPROPI signals and four optional NTCs through **SN74LV4051A → buffer → Pico GP27**. Put VM monitoring separately on GP26.
- Use a **TCA9535 I²C expander** for the four-bit channel selector, activity LEDs, and individual driver-fault identification.
- Keep waveform generation, current-limit references, ARM, watchdog heartbeat, and aggregate fault detection on **native Pico GPIO**.
- Add a **TPS3430 watchdog and shutdown latch**, independent of PIO/DMA. A timeout must require deliberate rearming.
- Build a **9–15 V board**, using a reverse-polarity P-MOSFET, fuse, SMBJ16A TVS, distributed bulk capacitance, and AP63205 buck. Treat 24 V as a separate power-section revision.
- Use **JST VH 2P for coils**, XH 2P for UART, and PH 2P for NTCs. Budget approximately **US$30–35 in parts per board**, including Pico and sockets, before manufacturing and shipping.

**[INF] Selection rationale.** DRV8874 combines the required independent disable, fault reporting, programmable current regulation, and current mirror at a reasonable price. Its exposed leads make inspection and rework easier than a leadless package, although its thermal pad still requires reflow-quality soldering.

DRV8876 is the practical fallback, not a guaranteed improvement in waveform fidelity. Its lower-current sensing is better: the published error below 0.15 A is ±7.5 mA, versus DRV8874’s ±30 mA below 0.4 A. Conversely, TI reported approximately **600 ns output-width jitter on one DRV8876 EVM** and recommended input pulses of at least approximately **800 ns** in that investigation. This is application evidence, not a guaranteed timing specification or a measured result for DRV8874. [DRV8876 electrical characteristics, p.6](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8876.pdf), [TI pulse-width investigation](https://e2e.ti.com/support/motor-drivers-group/motor-drivers/f/motor-drivers-forum/791567/drv8876-input-pulse-width-to-ensure-detection).

**[INF] The PWM-resolution distinction matters:**

| Carrier | Period | Ideal 10-bit time increment | Ideal 11-bit time increment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 kHz | 25 µs | 24.4 ns | 12.2 ns |
| 62.5 kHz | 16 µs | 15.6 ns | 7.81 ns |

An 11-bit duty adjustment near 50% does **not** require an isolated 8 ns pulse: it moves an edge on an approximately 8 µs pulse. Minimum pulse width primarily restricts operation near 0% and 100%; delay mismatch, dead time, jitter, and current chopping determine usable waveform accuracy elsewhere.

For example, 500 ns and 800 ns minimum pulses exclude approximately the outer **3.125%** and **5%** of the duty range at 62.5 kHz. Start characterization inside **10–90% duty**, then determine the usable range experimentally.

**[INF] Driver configuration and current limits:**

- Strap **PMODE high** for IN1/IN2 PWM control. Do not leave it floating: independent half-bridge mode disables internal current regulation.
- Initially leave **IMODE high impedance**, selecting fixed-off-time regulation with latched OCP response.
- Fixed-off-time operation is suitable for long note-frequency drive intervals. Cycle-by-cycle regulation waits for another input edge after tripping, so it is unsuitable as the default for low-frequency square-wave operation.
- Leave configuration-resistor footprints for later experiments, but do not make mode selection dependent on the I²C expander.

The mode behaviors are documented in the driver’s control/regulation tables; TI also distinguishes ordinary current chopping from destructive-overcurrent protection. [DRV8874 §§7.3.2–7.3.3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8874.pdf), [TI current regulation versus OCP explanation](https://e2e.ti.com/support/motor-drivers-group/motor-drivers/f/motor-drivers-forum/850885/faq-overcurrent-protection-ocp-vs-current-regulation).

Use **RIPROPI = 4.99 kΩ, 1%**, connected directly from IPROPI to quiet ground. Using the specified nominal mirror ratio of 450 µA/A:

\[
I_{\rm trip}\approx \frac{V_{\rm REF}}{0.000450\times4990}
                 =\frac{V_{\rm REF}}{2.2455}
\]

| R_DOWN assembly option | Maximum nominal VREF | Maximum nominal current threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| **6.8 kΩ — initial build** | 1.336 V | **0.595 A** |
| **20 kΩ — qualified higher-current option** | 2.200 V | **0.980 A** |

With 20 kΩ, the filter time constant is approximately 1.47 ms. Allow approximately 10 ms after changing a reference before enabling a previously disabled channel.

These are **nominal thresholds**, not absolute peak guarantees. Include mirror error, resistor/supply tolerance, blanking, and overshoot. If 1 A is an absolute coil limit, use a lower nominal setting and establish the required margin on the bench.

For the DRV8876 variant, use approximately **2.21 kΩ RIPROPI** because its nominal mirror ratio is 1000 µA/A; recalibrate firmware. A shared VREF would unnecessarily couple four different coils’ limits and volumes.

## 4. Sensing and ADC architecture

**[INF] Recommend the analog mux architecture for the first board.** Its purpose is calibration, current-envelope monitoring, and temperature supervision. It is not a four-channel precision current-control acquisition system.

```text
Each IPROPI:
    IPROPI ---- 4.99 kΩ ---- GND       current-regulation path
       |
       +---- TLV9004 voltage follower
                    |
                  10 kΩ
                    |
                    +---- mux input 0..3
                    |
                  100 nF
                    |
                   GND

NTC dividers ------------------------ mux inputs 4..7

SN74LV4051A common --> TLV9001 follower --> 100 Ω --> GP27
                                                       |
                                                      1 nF
                                                       |
                                                      GND
```

The quad buffer prevents the monitoring filter from loading or delaying the IPROPI feedback node. The four 10 kΩ/100 nF filters have approximately **159 Hz bandwidth**. Provide raw IPROPI test points and optional unpopulated filter-capacitor positions for faster characterization.

**[OBS]** SN74LV4051A operates from low-voltage logic supplies; TLV900x provides rail-to-rail input/output, unity-gain-stable buffering. Keep all mux analog signals within its supply rails. [SN74LV4051A recommended conditions](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lv4051a.pdf), [TLV900x datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tlv9001.pdf).

| Architecture | Acquisition capability | GPIO consequence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pico ADC + LV4051A** | Pico ADC: 500 kS/s aggregate; proposed filters intentionally limit monitoring bandwidth | 3 mux address pins + GP27; VM on GP26 | **Recommended:** inexpensive, adequate diagnostics |
| CD4051 / 74HC4051 | Adequate for slow measurements; greater supply-dependent switch resistance requires settling care | Same as above | Usable, but prefer the LV device at 3.3 V |
| **MCP3208 at 3.3 V** | Conservatively design around the 50 kS/s specification at 2.7 V; 100 kS/s is specified at 5 V | SPI GP16–19 can replace mux selection; frees GP27 | Good if repeatable external ADC behavior is worth approximately $3–5 estimated |
| **ADS7828 at 3.3 V** | Approximately 8 kS/s at 400 kHz I²C; 50 kS/s requires 3.4 MHz high-speed I²C | Shares GP20/21 | Suitable for slow monitoring; approximately $4–8 estimated |
| Inline shunts + PWM-rejecting amplifiers + faster ADC | Can measure signed winding current through regeneration | Larger BOM and acquisition workload | Reserve for accurate RMS/current-waveform requirements |

**[OBS]** ADC rates above are from [RP2040 §4.9](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf), [MCP3208 electrical characteristics](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/devicedoc/21298e.pdf), and [ADS7828 throughput table](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7828.pdf). RP2040’s nominal 12-bit ADC is characterized at approximately **8.7 effective bits**.

**[INF] Sampling plan:**

- Sample filtered current channels at approximately **500–1000 samples/s/channel**, then report envelopes at 20–50 Hz.
- Read NTCs and VM at approximately **10–20 Hz**.
- Initially allow **20–50 µs settling after mux selection**, discard the first conversion, and confirm settling experimentally.
- For waveform characterization, remove the relevant monitoring filter capacitor and acquire a burst from **one selected channel**.
- Four simultaneous per-carrier peak measurements need at least **250 k measurements/s at 62.5 kHz**, plus correctly timed acquisition windows. Neither a slow I²C ADC nor an ordinary mux scan establishes that capability.

**[OBS]** IPROPI is not a universal signed winding-current output. It senses particular low-side conduction paths, misses coast/regenerative intervals, and has a sense-valid delay. Drive/brake operation offers more favorable sensing continuity than some complementary switching conditions. [DRV8876 §7.3.3.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8876.pdf).

**[INF] Therefore, do not calculate coil heating simply as \(R(\text{filtered IPROPI})^2\).** Mean current magnitude is not RMS current, and missing conduction intervals introduce additional error. Use calibrated operating envelopes and temperature measurements.

If accurate signed current becomes essential, an optional **0.1 Ω inline shunt + INA240A1**, referenced around 1.65 V, gives approximately 2 V/A. Its PWM rejection and extended common-mode range suit bridge outputs, but add roughly $2–4 per channel plus ADC/filter costs. [INA240 features and gain options](https://www.ti.com/product/INA240).

**[INF] NTC recommendation:** populate the connectors and divider footprints now; initially fit sensors to all four experimental drives.

- 10 kΩ NTC, defined B-value such as B3950; use its actual resistance table.
- 10 kΩ, 1% pull-up to 3.3 V, connector-series resistance, and a local filter capacitor.
- PH 2P connector; detect open/short conditions.
- Prefer a location thermally coupled to the coil/former. A sensor on nearby stationary metal has substantial uncertainty and lag.
- Any sensor wiring on the moving assembly must be sufficiently light and flexible.
- The draft’s approximately 60°C threshold is a provisional project limit, not an established HDD-coil rating.

**[INF] Capacitor sizing and regeneration:**

- In locked-antiphase operation, bridge supply current can alternate between approximately \(+I\) and \(-I\). Local capacitor ripple can approach **the coil current RMS**, rather than half that value.
- Four synchronized bridges can add coherently. Do not assume their ripple sums as \(\sqrt{4}\).
- A representative Panasonic **EEUFR1V471L** is rated at 1.56 A ripple at 100 kHz; apply its frequency/temperature corrections for the actual conditions. [Panasonic component specification](https://na.industrial.panasonic.com/products/capacitors/aluminum-electrolytic-capacitors/lineup/aluminum-electrolytic-capacitors-radial-lead-type/series/83367/model/83837).
- At 1 A, an ideal 470 µF capacitor changes approximately **17 mV over 8 µs**; ESR and layout inductance add further ripple.
- Four 4 mH coils at 1 A contain **8 mJ magnetic energy**. Returning that to 2350 µF initially at 12 V raises the ideal voltage to approximately **12.28 V**. Mechanical energy and cable inductance are additional.
- The TVS is a transient clamp, not a continuous regenerative brake resistor or protection against indefinitely connecting the wrong adapter.

A 12 V/3 A adapter is reasonable for the stated typical currents. Four simultaneous sustained 1 A channels are not a supported operating assumption. Also, a current-limited 3 A adapter may never blow a 3.15 A fuse; protection must not depend on that fuse clearing every fault.

**24 V option:** the bridges permit it, but this BOM does not. The TVS, buck margin, capacitors, connector ratings, and regeneration limits must be reconsidered together.

**[INF] PWM implementation constraints:**

- Each bridge occupies one complete A/B slice.
- Locked-antiphase: equal A/B compare values, with B inverted. Update both compare fields together.
- Sign-magnitude with slow decay: hold one input high and PWM the other, alternating between drive and `11` brake. Swap roles for reverse direction.
- Reference pairs share frequency but retain independent duty values. Run all four reference PWMs at the same fixed carrier.
- At a 125 MHz system clock, edge-aligned PWM with divider 1 and **TOP=1999 gives 62.5 kHz and 2000 timing counts**, approximately 10.97 bits.
- **TOP=2047 gives 61.035 kHz and exactly 2048 counts**.
- Use PIO for independent note-frequency square waves; do not retune a reference-sharing PWM slice accidentally.

**Critical off-state detail:** with B inverted, setting PWM duty to zero produces one high bridge input. It is **not OFF**. The shutdown routine must first lower ARM, then stop DMA/PIO and explicitly return bridge pins to `00`.

## 7. Fail-safe hardware and UART interface

**[OBS]** SmartNet §2 specifies master-side 100 Ω, slave-side **10 kΩ pull-up**, and a two-wire TX/GND connection. Section 3 requires shutdown within 500 ms after the last valid frame, reset-safe output pulls, and watchdog operation. [Local SmartNet specification](C:/Users/ktrog/Desktop/役に立たない回路コンテスト/参考/SmartNet_v0.3.5_仕様書.md).

**[INF] UART implementation:**

```text
XH bus TX -----+---------------- raw pass-through TX
               |
               +-- 1 kΩ -- 74LVC1G17 -- 100 Ω -- GP1
               |
3V3 -- BAS70 -- 10 kΩ
       -->
```

Place the pull-up on the bus side of the local 1 kΩ resistor. The BAS70 is oriented to supply pull-up current from 3.3 V while blocking reverse current into an unpowered local rail. Add buffer decoupling and an optional low-capacitance, ground-referenced ESD device.

**[OBS]** 74LVC1G17 supports partial power-down using Ioff. **[INF]** That feature protects the buffer path; it does not isolate an ordinary pull-up resistor connected directly to an unpowered supply. [SN74LVC1G17 datasheet, Ioff feature](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g17.pdf).

**[INF] Hardware shutdown implementation:**

1. Fit **10 kΩ external pull-downs** on all eight bridge inputs, ARM, and common nSLEEP.
2. Use **TPS3430WDRCR** with both supply pins at 3.3 V, **CWD through 10 kΩ to 3.3 V**, SET0 low, SET1 high, and CRST unconnected.
3. Feed falling edges every approximately **50 ms**, generated by the healthy main control task.
4. Connect WDO to the asynchronous clear of **SN74LVC1G74**. Tie D and preset high; ARM drives its clock.
5. Generate common nSLEEP with **SN74LVC1G08: ARM AND latch-Q**.
6. Add STOP directly to latch clear.
7. Combine the four nFAULT signals through two **BAT54A common-anode diode pairs** into the same active-low clear net. Preserve individual fault signals for expander readback.
8. Pull the clear net up to 3.3 V and observe it on GP22.

**[OBS]** For this TPS3430 configuration, the valid heartbeat window lies between **10.35 ms maximum lower boundary** and **165.8 ms minimum upper boundary**; the latest timeout is **224.3 ms**. Its fault output is pulsed, which is why the separate latch is useful. [TPS3430 §6.6 and Table 8-3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3430.pdf). The selected flip-flop provides asynchronous clear and Ioff behavior. [SN74LVC1G74 functional description](https://www.ti.com/product/SN74LVC1G74).

**[INF] Link-loss timing policy:**

- Refresh the watchdog only while the control task is healthy and valid-frame age is **less than 200 ms**.
- Hardware timeout then occurs no later than approximately **200 + 224.3 = 424.3 ms** after the last valid frame, leaving margin for driver disable.
- Also perform ordinary firmware shutdown by approximately **400 ms**.
- Never generate WDI with autonomous PWM, PIO, DMA, or an interrupt that continues despite a failed control task.
- After a timeout or driver fault, require ARM low, fault checks, healthy watchdog service, settled references, and a deliberate ARM rising edge.
- Keep RP2040’s internal watchdog enabled as a separate CPU recovery mechanism.

The startup routine must wait for the watchdog startup/reset interval to finish before arming. Neither a driver fault nor the watchdog’s later release should automatically restart a note.

## 8. PCB layout and manufacturing

**[INF] Layout recommendation:**

- Target approximately **100 × 80–100 mm**, two layers, 1.6 mm FR-4, 1 oz copper.
- Place the four bridges along the coil-connector edge, each with adjacent ceramics and its own bulk capacitor.
- Keep the input connector, fuse, reverse MOSFET, TVS, and central bulk together.
- Put the buck and its switch node away from VREF, current sensing, NTC inputs, and Pico ADC pins.
- Use a **continuous ground plane**. Adapt the draft’s “star ground” intent through placement and return-path control; do not split the PCB ground plane into long isolated analog/power branches.
- Return each bridge’s switching loop to its own decoupling capacitor. Connect RIPROPI and analog reference returns locally with quiet/Kelvin routing.
- Use broad VM/return pours. Starting widths of roughly 2–3 mm for individual bridge paths and 5–8 mm for the power trunk are practical where space permits; verify temperature rise and neck-downs.
- Solder every exposed pad. Follow the manufacturer land pattern, segmented paste guidance, and a small thermal-via array. Avoid large open holes that drain solder beneath the package.
- Keep OUT traces away from UART and analog nodes; provide approximately 22–100 Ω input-series-resistor footprints for edge/ringing experiments.
- Twist each coil’s two wires together. Neither output is ground.
- Do not place a conventional one-way flyback diode across the bipolar coil. Optional RC snubbers should be DNP until measured ringing justifies them.

## 10. Bring-up and validation before PCB commitment

All steps are **[INF]**.

1. **Characterize the actual drives.** Measure coil resistance, inductance versus arm position where practical, insulation from chassis, and the original flex connection. Confirm that the old HDD electronics are disconnected from the coil.

2. **Build one DRV8874 channel first.** Use an EVM or a properly laid-out breakout with a soldered exposed pad and local decoupling. An AE-TB67H450 prototype can establish mechanical/acoustic behavior, but cannot qualify DRV8874 PWM timing.

3. **Verify the current threshold.** Begin with a current-limited supply and low VREF. Test 9, 12, and 15 V; approximately 0.1, 0.3, and 0.5 A thresholds; and the higher-current option only after establishing coil limits. Measure overshoot, not just average current.

4. **Test both waveform styles.** Exercise 30 Hz–2 kHz bipolar square waves, then 40/61/62.5 kHz carriers. Compare locked-antiphase and drive/brake sign-magnitude operation.

5. **Measure PWM fidelity directly.** Observe IN1/IN2, differential coil voltage, and actual coil current. Sweep duty near 50% in single timer-count steps, near zero output, and toward the rails. Check monotonicity, dead zones, jitter, DC bias, and current-chopping interaction. Use a differential/current probe arrangement suitable for floating bridge outputs.

6. **Calibrate current sensing.** Compare IPROPI with a current probe or correctly instrumented inline shunt in drive, brake, coast, and regenerative conditions. Decide which measurements firmware can use reliably.

7. **Establish thermal operating limits.** Run representative musical passages and sustained worst-case notes. Correlate NTC location/lag with coil heating. Establish continuous current, peak duration, derating, and shutdown thresholds.

8. **Prove fail-safe timing.** Disconnect UART, send invalid frames, freeze the main task while PIO/DMA continues, press RUN, enter BOOTSEL, remove the Pico, and test VM-only, USB-only, and simultaneous USB/adapter power. Measure time from the last valid frame to bridge disable and confirm no automatic restart after watchdog release.

9. **Test power transients and four-channel loading.** Measure VM during adapter removal, note release, reversal, and synchronized PWM. Verify buck regulation, reverse-PMOS temperature, connector temperature, capacitor ripple, and adapter current limiting.

10. **Release the PCB only after the one-channel results.** Check exact MPN/package footprints, exposed-pad soldering, capacitor lead spacing, Pico socket orientation, connector incompatibility, and the complete timeout/rearm sequence. Recheck assembly stock immediately before BOM freeze.

## 11. Risks and open decisions

| Issue | Consequence / required decision |
|---|---|
| **Effective PWM fidelity remains unproven** | Approve DRV8874 for prototype qualification, not an unconditional claim of 11-bit analog performance. |
| **Actual VCM parameters and thermal capacity are unknown** | Current settings must be drive-specific. Hardware current limiting protects against excessive current, not every possible overheating condition. |
| **12 V voltage compliance** | Ignoring losses, a 40 Ω coil cannot reach 0.5 A from 12 V; 1 A requires a sufficiently low-resistance coil. Inductance/back-EMF further reduce high-frequency current. |
| **Current chopping changes the waveform** | In synthesis mode, set normal waveform amplitude below the protective threshold; characterize behavior when the limit engages. |
| **IPROPI is not complete RMS sensing** | Do not promise accurate heating estimation from the proposed slow current channels alone. |
| **Regenerative energy** | Verify the actual adapter/board combination; TVS pulse capability is finite. |
| **NTC mounting** | Sensor lag and added moving mass may be significant. A nearby stationary sensor is only an indirect estimate. |
| **SmartNet channel semantics** | Decide whether the DIP selects one four-voice instrument channel or a base channel for four independent channels. Hardware supports either firmware interpretation. |
| **Shutdown recovery policy** | Require deliberate rearming and fresh valid state; do not resume stale notes after a fault/reset. |
| **24 V support** | Excluded from this BOM; requires coordinated power-section changes. |
| **Expansion beyond four VCMs** | Requires GPIO/reference reassignment and a revised power budget. |
| **Assembly and sourcing** | Exposed-pad solder quality and exact capacitor/package availability remain release checks. |
