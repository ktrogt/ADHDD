## 最終報告（Codex）

## 1. Top 5 issues to fix before building

1. **The “hardware guarantees ≤0.64 A” claim is false as written:** this is a nominal chopping threshold, with tolerances and transient overshoot; coil safety remains unproven.
2. **The shutdown deadline is incomplete:** a hang near lease expiry can extend energization toward 700 ms; partial power and pending callbacks also need defined handling.
3. **Validate low-current chopping before committing to this driver:** peak current is not average force or RMS heating current; the proposed resistor test cannot establish either.
4. **Correct the motion/control model:** quarter-period starting works ideally, but restart direction, changing notes, current tails, and mechanical forces invalidate the current guarantees.
5. **Finish power protection coordination:** the proposed TVS can clamp above the capacitor rating, and neither the 2 A estimate nor fuse coordination is established.

## 3. Math check of §4.4 / §6.2

**[INF] Ideal model:** constant acceleration magnitude \(a=F/m\), or angular acceleration \(a=K_tI/J\); no friction, spring torque, back-EMF or current transient. Initially \(x=v=0\).

For a full positive half-period followed by a negative half-period:

\[
v(T)=0,\qquad
\Delta x=\frac{aT^2}{8}+\frac{aT^2}{8}
=\boxed{\frac{aT^2}{4}}.
\]

Thus zero mean force does **not** imply zero displacement per period from this initial condition. The draft’s drift calculation is correct.

Starting instead with \(+a\) for \(T/4\), then \(-a\) for \(T/2\), then alternating full half-periods:

| Time | Position | Velocity |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(T/4\) | \(aT^2/32\) | \(aT/4\) |
| \(T/2\) | \(aT^2/16\) | \(0\) |
| \(3T/4\) | \(aT^2/32\) | \(-aT/4\) |
| \(T\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

This is periodic without ideal drift. However, its centre is **\(aT^2/32\) away from the starting position**. The initial position is a turnaround.

With phase-correct mode off, \(N=TOP+1\), CC_A=CC_B=N/2 and B inverted, starting CTR=N/4 correctly leaves a first positive pulse of T/4. Ideal zero-velocity points then occur at counter phases N/4 and 3N/4, assuming exact quarters.

Stopping there leaves the arm at rest **only if force disappears immediately**. Actual winding current and mechanical forces prevent that being guaranteed from the counter alone.

**Restart bookkeeping:** remember the force polarity immediately before the chosen zero-velocity stop. Use that same polarity for the next initial quarter pulse—it points back into the previous excursion. Equivalently, reverse the original starting direction only if stopping at the far turnaround. After emergency shutdown, reset or impact, mark position/phase unknown.

Frequency or amplitude changes also break the ideal initial condition. For example, a frequency change at the counter wrap leaves velocity \(-aT_\text{old}/4\), whereas the new periodic motion requires \(-aT_\text{new}/4\). Their difference creates drift. Register buffering cannot correct this mechanical mismatch.

**Verdict:** the basic quarter-pulse derivation is correct; the claims of oscillating around the initial position, guaranteed quiet stopping and unrestricted in-place note changes are not.

## 4. Anything not verified

- Actual junk-drive resistance, inductance, torque constant, inertia, travel, friction, flex-cable torque, acoustic output and safe winding temperature.
- TB67H450 low-current waveforms and reversal behavior on these coils. The published accuracy specifications do not establish performance at the proposed settings.
- Actual firmware implementation, watchdog feeding, reset configuration, timer cancellation or SmartNet integration; only specifications were available.
- Exact buck, fuse, diode, capacitor and TVS selections, including adapter short-circuit behavior and protection coordination.
- All historical R/L measurements and the cited shutter paper’s overheating claim were not independently reproduced from complete primary texts. Calculations above using those R/L values treat them as illustrative inputs.
- Current shop availability/prices and model-specific HDD disassembly details were not comprehensively audited.
