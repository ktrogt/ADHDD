[OBS] A separate patent reports measurements from a roughly 3.5-inch-sized HDD actuator: \(R=36.8\,\Omega\), \(L=3.7\,{\rm mH}\), and \(K_e=0.139\,{\rm V\,s/rad}\). It estimates rotor inertia at 19.0 g·cm² and assumes arm inertia of 19.5 g·cm². [Original measurement description](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2000044084A2/en)

[INF] Interpreting those inertia terms as additive gives \(J\approx3.85\times10^{-6}\ {\rm kg\,m^2}\). In consistent SI units, \(K_t=K_e\). These examples justify an order-of-magnitude model; they do not bound every modern actuator.

**[INF] Mechanical and electrical model**

\[
J\ddot\theta+b\dot\theta+k_\theta\theta=K_t i
\]

\[
v=Ri+L\frac{di}{dt}+K_e\dot\theta.
\]

[INF] The ideal free-mass calculation below sets \(b=k_\theta=0\) and assumes the specified **current waveform actually flows**. A commanded voltage waveform generally produces a different current waveform.

**What radiates sound**

[OBS] HDD acoustic research identifies structure-borne transmission from the actuator assembly to the cover, plus airborne transmission inside the drive. A Seagate patent specifically describes reaction forces at the magnet assembly exciting the chassis and lid. [Acoustic study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224607907_Modeling_and_Prediction_of_Structure-Borne_Seek_Noise_of_Hard_Disk_Drives), [reaction-force description](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2005500635A/en)

[INF] For this instrument, three mechanisms should be distinguished:

- **Chassis/panel radiation:** reaction torque and bearing forces excite the chassis, lid, and attached soundboard.
- **Direct arm radiation:** possible, but the small, largely in-plane moving structure has little effective radiator area.
- **Impacts:** abrupt contact excites many structural modes, producing loud clicks and ringing rather than a clean sustained pitch.

[INF] With the nominal model and \(I_{\rm pk}=0.20\) A, torque amplitude is 0.0184 N·m—equivalent to a tangential force of approximately 0.61 N at a 30 mm radius. Significant reaction excitation can therefore coexist with barely visible arm motion.

[INF] **At constant current, torque and ideal arm acceleration stay constant while displacement falls as \(f^{-2}\). This does not imply SPL falls as \(f^{-2}\).** Sound depends on structural modes, radiation efficiency, mounting, and direction. Raising high-frequency excursion to match low-frequency excursion would demand current proportional to \(f^2\), an unsuitable control objective.

**Periodic excursion**

[INF] Let \(\alpha=K_t I_{\rm pk}/J\). For the nominal example, \(\alpha=2876\ {\rm rad/s^2}\).

For a correctly initialized sinusoidal current:

\[
\theta_{\rm pk}=\frac{\alpha}{(2\pi f)^2},
\qquad x_{\rm pp}=2r\theta_{\rm pk}.
\]

For a correctly initialized bipolar square current:

\[
\theta_{\rm pp}=\frac{\alpha}{16f^2},
\qquad x_{\rm pp}=r\theta_{\rm pp}.
\]

[INF] All values assume **0.20 A peak**, not RMS. Excursion scales directly with \(K_t/J\), current, and tip radius. For an excursion-limited channel, the low-frequency current ceiling should decrease approximately as \(f^2\).

**Why zero average current does not guarantee no drift**

[INF] Starting from rest, after a finite excitation lasting \(T\):

\[
\dot\theta(T)=\frac{K_t}{J}\int_0^T i(t)\,dt
\]

\[
\theta(T)-\theta(0)=
\frac{K_t}{J}\int_0^T(T-t)i(t)\,dt.
\]

[INF] Zero current integral removes final velocity. Returning to the original position also requires the weighted integral—the first moment—to vanish.

[INF] A square current beginning with \(+I\) for \(T/2\), then \(-I\) for \(T/2\), returns velocity to zero but advances position by:

\[
\Delta\theta=\frac{\alpha T^2}{4}.
\]

At 100 Hz in the nominal example, this is **4.12° per cycle** in the ideal undamped current-driven model.

[INF] The draft’s quarter-period start addresses this: the balanced cell

\[
+I\text{ for }T/4,\quad -I\text{ for }T/2,\quad +I\text{ for }T/4
\]

has both zero impulse and zero first moment. Its boundaries occur at zero velocity. This explains why stopping at the middle of a current pulse can work.

[INF] However, inductance, back-EMF, current chopping, and current-decay tails shift the real zero-velocity point. A quarter-period **logic** pulse is therefore a useful starting strategy, not a position guarantee.

[INF] For a sinusoidal current switched on from rest,

\[
i=I\sin(\omega t+\phi)
\]

\[
\dot\theta=
\frac{\alpha}{\omega}
\left[\cos\phi-\cos(\omega t+\phi)\right].
\]

The constant velocity component is \(\alpha\cos\phi/\omega\). Consequently:

- [INF] Starting at a sine zero crossing, \(\phi=0\), maximizes this ideal drift component. At 100 Hz, displacement advances **2.62° per cycle**.
- [INF] Starting at a current maximum, \(\phi=\pi/2\), removes that component, but abruptly applies torque and can create a click.

**Attack/release envelopes**

[INF] A linear amplitude ramp over exactly \(N\) whole periods removes the residual constant-velocity term after the ramp in the ideal sine/current model. It can still leave a position offset.

[INF] A smoother raised-cosine attack is not automatically drift-free. For a zero-crossing sine start, its residual velocity relative to the abrupt-start value is:

\[
-\frac{1}{4N^2-1}.
\]

That is approximately −1.59% for four periods and −0.392% for eight periods: much smaller, but not zero.

[INF] For the square fallback, changing amplitude only between complete balanced cells preserves the ideal zero-impulse/zero-first-moment property. Arbitrarily multiplying the waveform by an envelope does not.

[INF] Practical requirement: smooth attacks and releases, preserve phase during ordinary modulation, suppress DC/subsonic content, and validate repeated note transitions. **Use passive restoring compliance to make arbitrary timbres robust; do not rely on DC removal alone.**

[INF] Voltage drive also supplies electromagnetic damping. Neglecting inductance at low frequency,

\[
b_e\approx\frac{K_tK_e}{R}.
\]

With the nominal constants and \(R=10\,\Omega\), the velocity transient time constant is approximately 7.6 ms. Thus the unlimited drift examples above are not literal predictions for every voltage-driven HDD. Nevertheless, damping provides no unique center position; a tight current servo removes much of this passive damping unless it deliberately restores it.

**Restoring force and position knowledge**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| [INF] Native flex-cable compliance | Test first. Retain it if the arm repeatedly returns to a safe central region and does not creep during music. |
| [INF] Weak opposed springs | Recommended next step when native centering is inadequate. Provide a defined center without a position-control firmware project. |
| [INF] Rubber/elastic thread | Useful prototype; creep, temperature dependence, and nonlinear stiffness make calibration less repeatable. |
| [INF] Potentiometer | Adds friction, inertia, and mechanical coupling. Poor first choice for these small motions. |
| [INF] Optical position sensing | Best upgrade if passive centering fails. A continuous sensor can support a slow centering loop; a single interrupter only establishes a reference or limit. |
| [INF] Back-EMF estimation | Useful for velocity estimation, but insufficient as the only position safeguard: back-EMF vanishes at rest, and integration accumulates error. |

[INF] Target a centering resonance well below the playable register:

\[
f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k_\theta}{J}}.
\]

For the nominal actuator, 10, 15, and 20 Hz require \(k_\theta=0.025,\ 0.057,\ 0.101\ {\rm N\,m/rad}\), respectively. At a 30 mm attachment radius, these correspond to combined tangential spring stiffnesses of approximately **28, 63, and 112 N/m**.

[INF] A 10–20 Hz, moderately damped centering system is a sensible starting target for a 131 Hz minimum note. A high-Q resonance within the musical register would disproportionately amplify particular notes and ring after note-off.

[INF] Back-EMF estimation requires separating \(Ri\), \(L\,di/dt\), and switching artifacts from terminal voltage. Across the supplied independent R/L bounds, waiting five electrical time constants takes approximately **88 μs–3.7 ms**—potentially disruptive to audio. Do not make this the first implementation.

**4. Requirements for the hardware designer**

**PWM architecture**

[OBS] RP2040 provides paired PWM outputs, wrap-paced DMA, and double-buffered PWM compare registers. [RP2040 datasheet](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)

[INF] Keep each bridge’s IN1/IN2 on the two outputs of one PWM slice. The draft’s GP2/3, GP4/5, GP6/7, and GP8/9 allocation supports this architecture.

[INF] At a 125 MHz system clock:

- 62.5 kHz carrier: `TOP=1999`, 2000 duty steps, approximately **10.97 bits**.
- 40 kHz carrier: `TOP=3124`, 3125 steps, approximately **11.61 bits**, but greater current ripple.
- Recommended audio rate: **31.25 ksample/s**, repeating each sample for two carrier periods.

[INF] A 48 ksample/s engine is possible, but combining it with 62.5 kHz PWM requires explicit resampling or timing management. Avoid irregular sample repetition. Sixteen-bit synthesis arithmetic is useful even though the output duty has approximately eleven bits; actual analog resolution will be lower.

**Locked-antiphase versus sign-magnitude**

[OBS] Locked-antiphase alternates positive and negative bridge voltage; sign-magnitude separates direction from magnitude. [TI explanation of the two modes](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/snoa170c/snoa170c.pdf)

[OBS] On TB67H450, `10` and `01` select opposite drive directions, `11` shorts the outputs through the low-side devices, and `00` makes them high impedance. [TB67H450 datasheet](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)

| Property | Locked-antiphase | Sign-magnitude with brake recirculation |
|---|---|---|
| [INF] Switching states | `10 ↔ 01` | `10 ↔ 11`, or `01 ↔ 11` |
| [INF] Ideal average voltage | \(V_M(2D-1)\) | \(\pm V_MD\) |
| [INF] Quiet-level behavior | Operates near 50% duty; avoids extremely short magnitude pulses | Very short pulses near zero can produce a dead zone or crossover distortion |
| [INF] Carrier ripple | Largest around zero audio command | Zero at zero command; lower worst-case ripple |
| [INF] Main drawback | Continuous idle ripple while enabled; more switching activity | Low-level nonlinearity needs measurement |
| [INF] Recommendation | First candidate for smooth quiet expression | Implement as an A/B alternative; select it if measured distortion is acceptable |

[INF] For sign-magnitude, use **drive/brake**, not an unexamined drive/coast substitution: recirculation behavior changes the relationship between duty, current, and back-EMF.

[INF] Approximate locked-antiphase ripple at zero audio command is:

\[
\Delta I_{\rm pp}\approx\frac{V_M}{2Lf_{\rm PWM}}.
\]

For sign-magnitude, maximum ripple is approximately half this:

\[
\Delta I_{\rm pp,max}\approx\frac{V_M}{4Lf_{\rm PWM}}.
\]

| 12 V carrier condition | Locked-antiphase ripple |
|---|---:|
| [INF] 62.5 kHz, 0.65 mH | 0.148 A peak-to-peak; 42.6 mA RMS |
| [INF] 62.5 kHz, 3.7 mH | 0.0259 A peak-to-peak; 7.49 mA RMS |
| [INF] 40 kHz, 0.65 mH | 0.231 A peak-to-peak; 66.6 mA RMS |

[INF] These assume approximately triangular ripple and neglect bridge imperfections. At 10.2 Ω/0.65 mH, the calculated additional copper loss is about **19 mW at 62.5 kHz**, versus 45 mW at 40 kHz. It is modest at strong output but significant relative to a quiet signal.

[INF] An ultrasonic carrier does not guarantee silence: nonlinear mechanics, current chopping, or beating can produce audible components. Disable the bridge at genuine idle instead of maintaining locked-antiphase switching indefinitely.

**TB67H450 current-limit interaction**

[OBS] The driver permits control-input frequencies up to 400 kHz. Its current comparator has approximately 3.6 μs blanking at switching; current-setting accuracy is specified at 1.5 A, not at the proposed tens of milliamps. [Datasheet](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)

[OBS] Current regulation uses mixed decay with approximately 25 μs fixed off-time; changing the reference does not immediately terminate an ongoing decay interval. [Original-part application note](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/TB67H450FNG_application_note_ja_20190510.pdf)

[INF] Therefore:

- Use VREF as a protection ceiling, with normal audio peaks and ripple below it.
- Do not treat this chopper as a precise audio current servo.
- Do not send sampled audio through the draft’s approximately 0.9 ms VREF RC filter.
- Measure low-current behavior. At 12 V/0.65 mH, the ideal current rise during 3.6 μs is approximately **66 mA**. Blanking is relevant to low thresholds, although it is **not** a minimum permitted external PWM pulse width.
- If normal playback repeatedly enters chopping, expect compression/distortion and possible audible modulation.

[INF] Voltage-mode drive with measured per-drive compensation is the recommended first implementation. A genuine current servo would improve waveform consistency, but requires signed current measurement, sufficient sampling rate, and demonstrated loop stability.

[INF] Optional feed-forward compensation is

\[
v^*=Ri^*+L\,di^*/dt,
\]

with voltage, current, and thermal limits. Prefer cutting strong acoustic resonances and modest bounded boosts; do not attempt to invert deep mechanical response notches.

**5. Firmware requirements**

**Synthesis and resource budget**

[INF] Implement four independent channels containing a phase accumulator, band-limited waveform, envelope, vibrato, calibrated gain, and output limiting. Use fixed-point arithmetic and a wider accumulator for mixing.

[INF] Start with rounded square and sine/triangle. Limit generated harmonics to the usable band, approximately 5–8 kHz initially. A naïve sampled square wave will alias; use band-limited tables or equivalent synthesis.

[INF] Suggested articulation starting points:

- Plucked/short lead: roughly 5–10 ms attack, 100–300 ms decay, short release.
- Sustained lead: roughly 10–30 ms attack, 20–80 ms release.
- Optional vibrato: 4–6 Hz, ±5–15 cents, introduced after the attack.
- In the drift-sensitive fallback, quantize transitions to suitable whole waveform cells rather than assuming arbitrary ADSR multiplication preserves balance.

[INF] Keep oscillator phase continuous during bend and vibrato. A real repeated NOTE_ON should articulate again from the current envelope level with a short transition. STATE should not reset phase, envelope, vibrato delay, or note age.

[INF] At 31.25 ksample/s, a 125 MHz core has **4000 cycles per four-channel audio frame**. Four oscillators require 125,000 voice updates/s; twelve oscillators require 375,000. An assumed 100–200 cycles per voice update consumes roughly 12.5–25 million cycles/s for four voices or 37.5–75 million for twelve. These are planning estimates requiring profiling; FM and filtering add work.

[INF] Allocate core 0 to UART parsing, CRC, state reconciliation, controls, and safety supervision; core 1 to audio generation and DMA buffers. Avoid per-sample floating-point transcendental functions.

[INF] Four 32-bit PWM-register writes at 62.5 kHz require approximately **1 MB/s** of DMA traffic. Four double buffers holding 64 audio samples, each repeated twice, need approximately **4 kB**. A 32–64-sample block lasts 1.02–2.05 ms.

[INF] Require bounded buffering latency, no allocation in the audio path, and buffer-freshness supervision. An underrun must not leave a nonzero duty frozen indefinitely. Emergency shutdown must stop peripheral ownership and force the bridge inputs off.

**Timbre palette**

| Timbre | Expected HDD character and loudness | Risk/requirement |
|---|---|---|
| [INF] Rounded bipolar square | Bright, recognizable mechanical/chiptune lead; harmonics can excite useful chassis modes. Good default to audition. | Limit upper harmonics and harsh resonances. At equal peak current it heats more than sine. |
| [INF] Sine | Clean sustained pitch; may sound weak where the mechanical response has a dip. Particularly useful with amplification. | Low harmonic excitation does not remove drift or heating requirements. |
| [INF] Triangle | Softer, flute-like or subdued electronic character; useful accompaniment. | Often a useful compromise between sine purity and square brightness; actual level remains drive-dependent. |
| [INF] Zero-DC pulse | Hollow/nasal chiptune color; adjustable harmonic balance. | Unequal positive/negative durations at equal amplitudes create DC. Correct amplitudes and normalize peaks. |
| [INF] Simple FM | Metallic/plucked/bell-like tones; potentially complementary to chassis ringing. | Start with modest index, about 0.3–1, and simple ratios. Control aliasing, peaks, and any DC component. |
| [INF] Filtered-noise burst | Snare/hi-hat-like texture without deliberately hitting stops. | Begin with roughly 500 Hz–5 kHz filtering and 10–60 ms bursts; suppress low-frequency wander and limit RMS current. |
| [OBS] Arm-strike percussion | Distinct clack/bang/bell sounds demonstrated by Floppotron. | [INF] Separate opt-in mode with bounded pulses and mechanical testing; unsuitable as normal pitched-note articulation. [Source](https://silent.org.pl/home/2022/06/13/the-floppotron-3-0/) |

[INF] For a pulse with positive duration fraction \(D\le0.5\), a zero-mean current shape is:

\[
+I \text{ for fraction }D,\qquad
-I\frac{D}{1-D}\text{ for the remainder}.
\]

At \(D=0.25\), levels are \(+I\) and \(-I/3\), with RMS \(0.577I\). Its lower RMS at the same peak must be considered when comparing loudness.

[INF] At equal peak current, square has a larger fundamental than sine; at equal RMS current, sine puts all energy into the fundamental. “Square is louder” therefore needs a stated normalization and an acoustic measurement.

**Polyphony on one VCM**

[INF] Mixing two or three oscillators is physically feasible when the electrical and mechanical system is approximately linear. Speaker-style HDD playback supports the feasibility of complex waveforms, but it does not establish clean chords at the proposed drive levels.

[INF] Three voices normalized to avoid worst-case peak overload each receive one-third amplitude, **−9.54 dB per voice**. Normalizing for equal total RMS instead gives approximately −4.77 dB per voice, but requires additional peak headroom.

[INF] Keep per-drive polyphony experimental. Nonlinear restoring forces, current clipping, and impacts produce intermodulation; separate HDD voices avoid some of that competition. Do not implement polyphony by merely interleaving full-strength note pulses.

[INF] Assign four configurable SmartNet channels to the four HDDs. Select unused channels in the team arrangement; do not assume channel 10 must be percussion. Default to last-note-priority mono per HDD, while maintaining the protocol’s complete logical note list.

[OBS] For SMF type 1, the supplied system maps tracks to SmartNet channels rather than preserving each event’s original MIDI channel. STATE is sent **one frame every 50 ms, round-robin**, not a complete refresh of every channel every 50 ms. [Local protocol](<C:/Users/ktrog/Desktop/役に立たない回路コンテスト/参考/SmartNet_v0.3.5_仕様書.md>)

[INF] STATE processing must distinguish logical held notes from physical voices:

- Common notes retain their existing musical state.
- Missing notes are released; newly discovered notes are started.
- Voice stealing must not cause every subsequent STATE to restart the stolen note.
- A completed one-shot remains logically known until note-off, preventing periodic re-triggering.
- Real NOTE_ON events remain genuine re-articulation events.

[OBS] STATE does not resend controller values. The protocol also specifies immediate ALL_OFF and a maximum 500 ms communication lease, refreshed by valid SYSTEM or owned-channel frames. [Local protocol](<C:/Users/ktrog/Desktop/役に立たない回路コンテスト/参考/SmartNet_v0.3.5_仕様書.md>)

[INF] Put a CC initialization preamble at each song/loop start; optionally repeat important values at phrase boundaries. Use a local lease around 350 ms, plus independent audio/core freshness monitoring. ALL_OFF, CH_OFF, and faults must bypass ordinary release tails.

[INF] At 38400 baud, 8N1, available throughput is 3840 bytes/s. A four-byte NOTE_ON occupies 1.04 ms; four successive note-ons span about 3.13 ms from first completion to last. This is acceptable for the initial instrument without timestamps. Generate vibrato locally; four channels each receiving one five-byte CC at 20 Hz already consume 400 bytes/s.

**6. Mechanics, loudness, and amplification**

[INF] Begin with four independently replaceable mounting panels, approximately **3–6 mm plywood and 20×30 cm**, as test articles rather than final acoustic dimensions. Rigidly attach the HDD using appropriate-depth mounting screws. Compare flat panels against shallow wooden boxes.

[INF] Avoid placing compliant isolation between HDD and soundboard when the purpose is to transfer vibration. Isolation beneath the whole instrument may help reduce unwanted table coupling.

[INF] Preserve the factory magnet/yoke structure and actuator geometry. Ensure heads do not scrape stationary platters. Keep stationary platters for appearance if clearance is reliable; remove them only when useful for clearance or demonstrated acoustic improvement. **Removing platters does not reduce actuator-arm inertia.**

[INF] A/B test the original lid, open chassis, and a clear noncontact guard. The lid changes stiffness and radiation; “lid off is louder” should not be assumed. Secure loose parts before adding damping.

[INF] Use small damping patches to tame objectionable narrow resonances after measurement. Blanket damping may remove the very chassis vibration needed for unamplified sound.

[INF] Do not add arm mass initially. It changes inertia, resonances, and clearance without guaranteeing greater reaction-driven sound. Provide spring attachment points on robust arm structure, away from the fragile head suspensions.

**Simple piezo pickup chain**

[OBS] Piezo sensors are high-impedance, capacitive sources; TI recommends JFET/CMOS input stages and distinguishes nearby voltage buffers from remote charge amplifiers. [Piezo signal-conditioning application note](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sloa033a/sloa033a.pdf)

[INF] Use:

**HDD chassis/panel → piezo disc → protected high-impedance buffer → level control → four-channel summing stage → powered speaker/PA.**

[INF] Practical starting requirements:

- One pickup per chassis or soundboard; audition several positions.
- At least 1 MΩ input impedance; approximately 2.2–10 MΩ is a useful design range.
- Short, shielded pickup leads and a quiet supply.
- Input series resistance and low-leakage clamps for impact transients.
- Adjustable gain after buffering; initially accommodate unity to approximately ×20.
- Buffered level controls and a summing stage with four-channel headroom.
- Starting EQ: high-pass approximately 80–120 Hz, low-pass 6–10 kHz, and cuts at dominant harsh resonances.

[INF] For an illustrative 20 nF piezo, 1 MΩ gives a loading corner around 8 Hz; a 10 kΩ mixer input gives approximately 800 Hz. Direct connection to a low-impedance input can therefore make the pickup sound very thin.

[INF] Keep this analog chain physically separated from the motor switching circuitry. If contact pickups sound excessively colored, compare a close microphone approximately 10–30 cm away. Neither method is immune to acoustic feedback; test speaker placement and mechanical isolation.

**7. Listening and measurement test plan**

| Stage | Procedure and decision |
|---|---|
| [INF] Characterize each drive | Measure R, L, free travel, restoring behavior, and actual signed current. Identify the hottest accessible coil/former location. Do not assume four same-model drives are identical. |
| [INF] Establish a safe baseline | Begin near 50 mA RMS around 400 Hz. Verify both polarities, idle behavior, and absence of scraping or impacts before widening frequency/current. |
| [INF] Map the playable register | Sweep semitone notes through approximately 100 Hz–2 kHz, reducing low-frequency current according to travel. Record current, spectrum, SPL, temperature, and center movement. |
| [INF] Compare PWM modes | A/B locked-antiphase and drive/brake sign-magnitude at silence, very quiet notes, normal level, and near the current ceiling. Examine whine, distortion, ripple, and chopper activation. |
| [INF] Compare timbres | Sine, triangle, square/rounded square, zero-DC pulse, FM, noise. First compare equal RMS current for heating/output efficiency; then compare equal acoustic level for preference. |
| [INF] Compare articulation | Abrupt versus shaped attacks; several release lengths; repeated notes, octave jumps, bend, and vibrato. Run at least 1000 transitions and look for progressive center movement. |
| [INF] Compare mechanics | Change one variable at a time: mounting panel, box, lid, platter configuration, centering, damping. Retain settings that improve both note balance and listening preference. |
| [INF] Evaluate polyphony | Compare a chord spread over three HDDs against the same chord mixed into one. Use sine pairs to expose intermodulation, then musical patches. Disable per-drive polyphony if clarity deteriorates. |
| [INF] Measure room coverage | Measure identical passages at 1 m and 5 m, fixed height and orientation, with background recorded. Use a calibrated meter or calibrated microphone; phone apps are suitable mainly for relative comparisons. |
| [INF] Conduct listening trials | Randomize level-matched recordings for several listeners. Score pleasantness, pitch clarity, harshness, rhythm, and voice balance. Include the actual contest arrangement. |
| [INF] Thermal endurance | Run sustained worst-case notes and the most demanding passage until temperature stabilizes or the provisional limit is reached. Repeat with all four channels and a warm restart. |
| [INF] Verify recovery | Drop NOTE_OFF, repeat unchanged STATE, lose a CC, disconnect UART, overload the bus, and simulate audio-buffer/core failure. Verify that resynchronization does not re-articulate existing voices. |

[INF] Proposed acceptance criteria: at least two useful octaves; tuning within ±5 cents; calibrated note-level spread preferably within 3–6 dB; no end-stop impacts during pitched playback; no progressive center walk; useful audible dynamics; and audience-position level above the agreed room-background target. Keep these as measured acceptance requirements, not assumed capabilities.

**8. Risks and open questions**

- [OBS] **Model-specific constants remain unverified:** the supplied documents do not establish R, L, \(J\), \(K_t\), thermal resistance, or restoring stiffness for the three candidates. [Draft and measurement notes](<C:/Users/ktrog/Desktop/役に立たない回路コンテスト/HDD楽器_仕様書.md>)
- [INF] **Raw loudness is the largest uncertainty.** Mounting and pickups should be tested before raising current or supply voltage.
- [INF] **TB67H450 audio quality is a bench question.** Quiet-level switching behavior and current-chopper interaction may determine which PWM mode is usable.
- [INF] **Quarter-period starting is conditional.** It solves an ideal waveform-initialization problem; it does not replace centering, current measurement, or transition testing.
- [INF] **A spring can improve positional robustness while worsening tone** if its resonance or nonlinear behavior enters the musical range.
- [INF] **Per-drive polyphony is optional.** Current headroom and mechanical linearity are more likely limitations than RP2040 arithmetic capacity.
- [INF] **STATE restores notes, not the complete synthesizer state.** Controller initialization and stable logical-note tracking are necessary.
- [OBS] **Not found:** calibrated music SPL for these candidate drives, controlled comparisons of HDD sine versus square versus FM, or convincing controlled tests of two/three-note polyphony and centering-spring acoustics. The linked build reports principally provide demonstrations and author experience.
- [INF] Before freezing the PCB, the highest-value experiment is **one HDD, one TB67H450, signed current measurement, a removable wooden panel, and a piezo buffer**. That experiment can settle the PWM mode, usable current, mounting approach, and need for amplification far more reliably than further specification alone.
