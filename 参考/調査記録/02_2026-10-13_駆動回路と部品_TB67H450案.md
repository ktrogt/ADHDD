## 指示文（Claude → Codex）

## Goal
A university student is building a "musical HDD instrument" (3-4 hard disk drives that play music from MIDI) for a school contest. The sound is made mainly by driving each HDD's head-positioning voice coil motor (VCM / actuator arm): either a square wave at the note frequency (tonal), or a short pulse that slams the arm into the crash stop (percussive). A Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) slave receives note commands over UART and must drive 4 VCM channels.

Your job is RESEARCH for the drive electronics (no files to write). Claude (the supervisor) will decide the final circuit and write the spec. I need sourced facts to choose: the driver IC/module per HDD, power supply, protection, and how to generate the waveforms from the Pico. Parts must be buyable in Akihabara (秋月電子通商, 千石電商, マルツ, aitendo, 若松通商, etc.) or easily online in Japan.

## Questions to answer
### Q1. Load characteristics (brief, just enough for design)
- Typical 3.5" HDD VCM coil resistance and inductance; 2.5" for comparison. Find a few measured values with sources. (Another agent researches HDD selection; you only need numbers for driver sizing.)
- Electrical time constant L/R and what it means for driving at audio frequencies (approx. 30 Hz - 2 kHz fundamental). Does current waveform become triangular at higher notes? Is loudness falling at high notes expected?

### Q2. Driver options - compare
For each option: part number, voltage range, continuous/peak current, logic-level compatibility with 3.3 V, PWM frequency limit, built-in current limiting/thermal shutdown, package (DIP / module / SMD needing a breakout), Akihabara shop + product code + price + URL (date of check), and pitfalls.
- Brushed-DC H-bridge ICs/modules: DRV8833, DRV8835, DRV8871, TB67H450FNG, TB6612FNG, BD6211/BD6231, L293D, L298N module, TA7291P (legacy), and any H-bridge module sold by 秋月 (e.g. 「DRV8835使用ステッピング&DCモータドライバモジュール」, 「TB67H450FNG使用 DCモータドライバモジュール」).
- Audio amplifier approach: drive the VCM as a speaker from a filtered PWM/DAC signal using a class-D or linear amp module (e.g., PAM8403/PAM8012, TPA2005/TPA3110/TPA3116 class-D, LM386, TDA2003/TDA7297/LM1875). Load impedance and DC-coupling concerns (VCM may need DC bias to center arm; amps are AC-coupled or bridge-tied-load). Is this approach used and worth it?
- Discrete: MOSFET H-bridge or single low-side MOSFET + flyback diode (only one direction - can it return the arm? arm springs back? (likely no spring; inference)).
- Recommend 1-2 options for: 4 channels, 12 V or 5 V supply, low cost, robust for a student, minimal SMD soldering.

### Q3. Waveform generation on the RP2040
- Using hardware PWM slices: one slice (A and B outputs, same frequency) can drive IN1/IN2 of one H-bridge with opposite polarity (channel B inverted via CSR PH_CORRECT/INVERT bits) - confirm via RP2040 datasheet (section PWM). With 8 slices and UART0 on GP0/GP1 (slice 0), how many independent H-bridge channels are possible and which pin pairs? Also note that in Arduino-Pico (Earle Philhower core) analogWrite / tone may conflict.
- Alternative: PIO state machines for tone generation; or a timer-interrupt-based approach. Pros/cons, jitter.
- Typical "drive-mode" schemes for H-bridge on a coil: sign-magnitude vs locked-antiphase, fast vs slow decay (coast vs brake) - which gives louder/cleaner tone on a VCM. Duty (pulse width) as volume control; high-frequency PWM carrier (e.g. 20-30 kHz) modulating a note-frequency envelope vs. direct square wave at note frequency.
- Percussion pulse: typical pulse width (ms) and voltage people use to make a strong click without damage; return pulse to pull the arm back.

### Q4. Power, protection, fail-safe
- Supply: 12 V AC adapter vs ATX PSU (the HDD's own Molex connector voltages 12 V / 5 V) - sizing for 4 channels; bulk capacitors per driver; separate ground return; decoupling.
- Coil overheating: thermal limit estimate (power dissipated = I^2 R at ~50% duty), recommend current limit (series resistor or driver current regulation e.g. DRV8871 ILIM, TB67H450 VREF).
- Back-EMF / inductive kick protection (most H-bridge ICs have body diodes - confirm) and TVS/bulk caps.
- Fail-safe requirement from the student's protocol spec: "driver inputs must have pull-down (or pull-to-OFF) resistors so the output is OFF while the MCU is booting/resetting or pins are inputs", and "all outputs OFF within 0.5 s of communication loss". Which driver ICs have internal pulldowns on IN pins / nSLEEP, and what external resistors are recommended.
- Level: Pico is 3.3 V logic; ensure driver VIH works at 3.3 V.

## 1. Summary and recommendation

- **[INF] First choice: four 秋月 AE-TB67H450 modules, one per HDD, with external current-sense resistors and adjustable VREF.** This gives bidirectional drive, current regulation, straightforward 3.3 V control, and no IC-level SMD soldering.
- **[OBS] The modules cost ¥320 each, ¥1,280 for four.** ICs are assembled; headers and external components need soldering. [秋月 114753](https://akizukidenshi.com/catalog/g/g114753/)
- **[INF] Prefer a regulated 12 V, 3 A adapter, with current initially limited to approximately 0.2 A per coil.** The extra voltage provides current-rise headroom; it must not become unrestricted 12 V across the coil. The same driver can support a 5 V version.
- **[INF] Second choice: two dual-channel AE-TB6612 modules at 5 V, with series resistors selected from measured coil resistance.** This saves only ¥280 in driver cost and loses adjustable current regulation, so the first option is preferable.
- **[OBS] DRV8835 is limited to 11 V operating supply, and 秋月’s module currently shows unavailable. DRV8871 requires at least 6.5 V.** Neither spans both requested supply options. [DRV8835 datasheet, §6.3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8835.pdf), [秋月 availability](https://akizukidenshi.com/catalog/g/g109848/), [DRV8871 datasheet, §6.3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8871.pdf)
- **[INF] Use four hardware PWM slices for the initial tonal implementation: GP2/3, GP4/5, GP6/7, GP8/9.** Generate complementary 50% outputs; implement OFF explicitly as both inputs LOW.
- **[INF] Treat 0.2 A and sub-millisecond percussion pulses as bring-up settings, not established safe ratings.** Unknown coil temperature limits and repeated crash-stop impacts remain the principal uncertainties.
- **[INF] Audio amplifiers are feasible for musical waveforms, but H-bridges offer simpler percussion, direction control, current limiting, and boot-time OFF behavior.**

### Electrical time constant

**[INF] Calculations from the two reported R/L pairs:**

| R / L | τ = L/R | Electrical corner R/(2πL) | Sinusoidal current at 2 kHz relative to low-frequency current |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8.516 Ω / 1.24 mH | 146 µs | 1.09 kHz | 0.480, approximately −6.4 dB |
| 10.2 Ω / 0.65 mH | 63.7 µs | 2.50 kHz | 0.781, approximately −2.2 dB |

- **[INF] At 30 Hz, these coils have ample time to approach their resistive current each half-cycle. At 2 kHz, the half-cycle is 250 µs, so current transitions become substantially rounded.**
- **[INF] A nearly triangular current requires the half-period to be much shorter than L/R. Thus “higher notes become triangular” is directionally correct, but 2 kHz is not necessarily deep into that regime for these examples.**
- **[INF] For an ideal stationary RL coil driven by ±V:**

  \[
  I_{\mathrm{peak}}=\frac{V}{R}\tanh\!\left(\frac{1}{4fL/R}\right)
  \]

  **[INF] At sufficiently high frequency this approaches \(V/(4fL)\). This excludes back-EMF, bridge losses, current regulation, and mechanical motion.**
- **[INF] Falling high-note output is plausible, but acoustic loudness cannot be predicted from L/R alone. Arm inertia, mechanical resonances, mounting, and stop impacts can dominate.**

- **[INF] Audio route:** Pico PWM → low-pass reconstruction filter → attenuation/AC coupling → amplifier → VCM. An external DAC is another option. Raw 3.3 V logic should not be treated as an appropriate full-scale analog input.
- **[INF] BTL itself does not prohibit differential DC. However, stock audio boards commonly use input coupling capacitors, and some ICs deliberately detect DC. They are therefore poor default choices for controlled DC bias or arbitrary long percussion pulses.**
- **[INF] A DC current provides torque, not a defined center position. Without a restoring mechanism or position feedback, “DC bias to center the arm” is not reliable.**
- **[INF] For single-ended amplifiers, output coupling capacitance matters at 30 Hz. With an 8 Ω load, 250 µF gives an approximately 80 Hz corner; 2,200 µF gives approximately 9 Hz.**
- **[OBS] BTL speaker terminals must remain separate; they cannot share a grounded “negative” terminal.** [PAM8012 manual p.2](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/AE-PAM8012_manual_20220831.pdf)

### Discrete alternatives

- **[INF] A MOSFET H-bridge is feasible, but adds high-side gate drive, level shifting, dead time, current sensing, fault handling, and layout work. Four ¥320 assembled IC carriers are a stronger student-build trade-off.**
- **[INF] A low-side MOSFET plus flyback diode can pull in only one direction. Use a MOSFET with RDS(on) specified at 2.5–3.3 V gate drive, rather than relying on its threshold voltage; approximately 100 Ω series gate resistance and 10 kΩ gate pull-down are reasonable starting values.**
- **[OBS] The published HDD-shutter mechanism had no internal spring or preferred arm position.** [Maguire et al., p.3078](https://bpb-ap-se2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.unimelb.edu.au/dist/5/118/files/2015/11/Maguire-Szilagyi-Scholten-2004-Review-of-Scientific-Instruments-High-performance-laser-shutter-using-a-hard-disk-drive-voice-coi-165h6mf.pdf)
- **[INF] Consequently, do not rely on passive return. Flex-cable torque, parking magnets, or mechanical rebound may exist in a particular drive, but repeatable return should use reverse current or an intentionally added restoring mechanism.**

## 4. RP2040 PWM / PIO plan

- **[OBS] RP2040 has eight PWM slices, each with A/B outputs sharing one counter and frequency. GPIO assignments repeat after GP15.** [RP2040 datasheet §4.5.1, Table 515, pp.521–522](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)
- **[INF] UART on GP0/GP1 does not consume PWM slice 0: those pins select the UART function, while slice 0 remains usable on GP16/GP17. Thus eight independent two-input H-bridge channels remain possible using GP2–GP17.**

| Assignment **[INF]** | Pin pair / slice **[OBS]** |
|---|---|
| HDD 1 | GP2 = PWM1A; GP3 = PWM1B |
| HDD 2 | GP4 = PWM2A; GP5 = PWM2B |
| HDD 3 | GP6 = PWM3A; GP7 = PWM3B |
| HDD 4 | GP8 = PWM4A; GP9 = PWM4B |
| Spare | GP10/GP11 = PWM5A/B |
| Spare | GP12/GP13 = PWM6A/B |
| Spare | GP14/GP15 = PWM7A/B |
| Spare despite UART on GP0/GP1 | GP16/GP17 = PWM0A/B |

**[OBS] Pin mapping source: [RP2040 Table 515](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf).**

- **[OBS] The inversion fields are `CSR.A_INV` bit 2 and `CSR.B_INV` bit 3. `PH_CORRECT` bit 1 selects phase-correct counting; it is not an inversion control.** [RP2040 §4.5.3, Table 517, p.531](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)
- **[INF] Initial setup:** free-running counter, phase-correct mode disabled, equal 50% compare values, B inverted. This alternates `10` and `01` into an IN/IN bridge.
- **[OBS] Pico SDK provides `pwm_config_set_output_polarity()` and per-slice configuration.** [Official SDK header](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raspberrypi/pico-sdk/master/src/rp2_common/hardware_pwm/include/hardware/pwm.h)
- **[INF] With phase-correct mode disabled:**

  \[
  f=\frac{f_{\mathrm{clk\_sys}}}{\mathrm{divider}\,(\mathrm{TOP}+1)}
  \]

  **[INF] At 125 MHz, divider 64 / TOP 65103 gives approximately 30 Hz; divider 1 / TOP 62499 gives exactly 2 kHz. Read the actual system clock rather than assuming 125 MHz. Integer dividers avoid fractional-divider edge modulation.**

- **[INF] OFF must force both pins LOW. Simply stopping the counter can leave a static driven polarity; setting complementary PWM duty to zero can also leave one input HIGH. Force GPIO outputs/overrides LOW before disabling the peripheral.**

### Arduino-Pico interactions

- **[OBS] Current upstream `analogWriteFreq()` maintains a global frequency setting and clamps frequencies below 100 Hz. `analogWrite()` can initialize an entire PWM slice with default settings.** [Current `wiring_analog.cpp`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earlephilhower/arduino-pico/master/cores/rp2040/wiring_analog.cpp)
- **[INF] Use the Pico SDK PWM functions directly, reserve the four slices, and avoid `analogWrite()` on their pins or aliases—for example GP18/19 share slice 1.**
- **[OBS] Current upstream Arduino-Pico `tone()` uses PIO state machines, not hardware PWM slices.** [Current `Tone.cpp`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earlephilhower/arduino-pico/master/cores/rp2040/Tone.cpp)
- **[INF] Therefore `tone()` is not automatically a PWM-slice conflict, but it can conflict over pins or available PIO resources. Verify the team’s installed core version. Two independent `tone()` calls are unsuitable for guaranteed complementary bridge timing.**

### Alternatives

- **[OBS] RP2040 provides two PIO blocks with four state machines each.** [RP2040 datasheet, Chapter 3](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)
- **[INF] One custom PIO state machine per HDD can generate `10`, `01`, and `00` with deterministic timing, including balanced pulse patterns and finite percussion sequences. Four channels fit comfortably. Ensure FIFO starvation cannot leave an indefinite driven state.**
- **[INF] Timer-interrupt toggling is feasible—four 2 kHz square waves need up to 16,000 edge events/s—but interrupt latency produces timing jitter. PWM/PIO are preferable when other firmware, USB, or critical sections run concurrently.**
- **[INF] Timestamped notes need a scheduler that changes the waveform at the requested time; UART parsing should not directly time individual waveform edges.**

## 5. Drive schemes: tone, percussion, volume

| Scheme | Findings / recommendation |
|---|---|
| **[INF] Direct note-frequency bipolar square wave** | Best initial implementation: equal-duration positive and negative drive, with current limited in hardware. Strong harmonics are expected. Zero average applied voltage does not guarantee a centered arm. |
| **[INF] Sign-magnitude PWM** | Direction selects polarity; carrier duty controls drive magnitude. For an IN/IN bridge, positive drive can use IN1=PWM, IN2=0, and reverse drive the opposite assignment. The off interval’s coast/brake choice changes current decay. |
| **[INF] Locked-antiphase PWM** | Complementary inputs switch between +VM and −VM. Average voltage is approximately `(2D−1)VM`; 50% gives zero average. For audio, vary duty about 50% using a high-frequency carrier. |
| **[INF] Volume with direct square waves** | Do **not** vary the complementary note-frequency duty away from 50% as a simple volume control: it introduces DC. Use equal-area positive/negative pulses with zero-drive intervals, an adjustable current reference, or carrier modulation. |
| **[INF] 20–30 kHz carrier** | Reasonable starting range for sign-magnitude or locked-antiphase synthesis. Four channels may share the carrier frequency while retaining independent note phases and amplitudes. Current ripple and mechanical response still need measurement. |
| **[INF] Fast versus slow decay** | Fast decay generally improves rapid current reduction/reversal; slow decay retains current with less ripple but can impair fast waveform tracking. Neither is universally louder on an HDD mechanism. |

- **[OBS] TB67H450 uses fixed mixed decay during current regulation: fast and slow portions within an approximately 25 µs off interval. Its application descriptions distinguish regenerative fast decay from local recirculation.** [Datasheet pp.8–9](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)
- **[OBS] For TB67H450, `00` means high-impedance stop, `11` means brake, and `10`/`01` select opposite directions.** [Datasheet p.6](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)
- **[OBS] TB67H450 specifies `00` intervals ≤0.7 ms for stop operation or ≥1.5 ms for standby; wake-up can take 30 µs.** [Datasheet pp.6, 15](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)
- **[INF] Firmware should accommodate that transition when starting/stopping notes, rather than assuming every short mute interval behaves identically.**

### Percussion

- **[OBS] The 2004 shutter paper used 30 V high-current pulses, including 1 ms and 9 ms examples, followed by much lower holding current. Its mechanism also used elastomer buffers.** [Paper pp.3078–3079](https://bpb-ap-se2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.unimelb.edu.au/dist/5/118/files/2015/11/Maguire-Szilagyi-Scholten-2004-Review-of-Scientific-Instruments-High-performance-laser-shutter-using-a-hard-disk-drive-voice-coi-165h6mf.pdf)
- **[OBS] The improved 2007 circuit demonstrated reverse-current braking after 3 ms, with 2 ms and 2.7 ms braking intervals.** [Scholten, Fig.4](https://www.holographyforum.org/data/pdf/aa-Collection_a_k/aa-electronic/shutter_rsi78_026101_2007.pdf)
- **[INF] There is no sourced universal “strong click without damage” setting. Begin around 0.5–1 ms at a measured 0.2 A current limit; increase only after observing travel and impact. A 2–5 ms exploratory range may be useful, but is not a validated safe range.**
- **[INF] Use a separately calibrated reverse pulse for return. A braking pulse during travel and a return pulse after impact serve different purposes. Avoid sustained current against a stop unless explicitly required and thermally tested.**

## 6. Power, protection, and fail-safe

### Supply and wiring

- **[INF] For four channels capped at 0.5 A peak, a regulated 12 V/3 A adapter gives reasonable margin for this VCM-only load.** This excludes spindle motors and other loads. Chopper-driver supply current is not identical to the sum of coil RMS currents.
- **[OBS] 秋月 lists a 12 V/3 A adapter, AD-A120P300, at ¥1,980.** [110661](https://akizukidenshi.com/catalog/g/g110661/)
- **[OBS] ATX peripheral connectors provide yellow +12 V, red +5 V, and black common returns.** [Intel ATX design guide, peripheral connector table](https://edc.intel.com/content/www/us/en/design/ipla/software-development-platforms/client/platforms/alder-lake-desktop/atx-version-3-0-multi-rail-desktop-platform-power-supply-design-guide/2.1/peripheral-connectors/)
- **[INF] An existing ATX supply is usable, but its large fault-current capability makes branch protection more important. The HDD’s original 12 V/5 V connector does not establish the bare VCM’s permissible voltage.**
- **[INF] Disconnect the coil from the HDD’s original driver electronics. Use short twisted coil leads and separate heavy driver returns to a common supply/bulk-capacitor ground point. Join Pico ground there; avoid carrying motor current through the Pico ground path.**
- **[INF] Power the Pico through its intended USB/5 V path. Do not connect the 12 V rail to it.**

### Coil heating and current limit

- **[INF] Use \(P_{\mathrm{coil}}=R\,\mathrm{mean}(i^2)\). Alternating +I and −I at 50% duty still produces approximately \(I^2R\), not half that power.**
- **[INF] For an 8 Ω coil, 0.2 A RMS gives 0.32 W, 0.3 A gives 0.72 W, and 0.5 A gives 2 W. Unrestricted 12 V across a stationary 10 Ω coil approaches 14.4 W before driver losses.**
- **[INF] Proposed TB67H450 starting components: \(R_S=0.47\,Ω\), 1 W, preferably 1%; adjustable VREF:**

  | Target threshold **[INF]** | VREF **[INF]** |
  |---:|---:|
  | 0.20 A | 0.94 V |
  | 0.30 A | 1.41 V |
  | 0.50 A | 2.35 V |

- **[INF] Set approximately 0.2 A first, verify actual current, and qualify sustained notes before raising it. Driver TSD protects the IC, not the coil.**
- **[OBS] Toshiba’s stated current-setting accuracy is characterized at 1.5 A; it is not a demonstrated ±5% guarantee for the proposed 0.2 A setting.** [TB67H450 datasheet p.12](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/info/TB67H450FNG_datasheet_en_20201126.pdf?did=65346&prodName=TB67H450FNG)
- **[INF] Resistor-limited alternative, assuming an 8 Ω coil:** at 5 V, 10 Ω series resistance gives approximately 0.28 A; use at least 2 W. At 12 V, 33 Ω gives approximately 0.29 A; use at least 5 W. Recalculate from the lowest measured coil resistance and actual supply maximum.

### Decoupling, regeneration, and fuses

- **[OBS] Toshiba recommends approximately 47–100 µF electrolytic plus 0.01–1 µF ceramic at VM; its typical ceramic value is 0.1 µF. It recommends short sense wiring, suitable ground routing, and supply fusing.** [Application note §§7.3–7.6, pp.10–11](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/TB67H450FNG_application_note_ja_20190510.pdf)
- **[INF] For this four-channel build, use 470 µF/35 V near each driver, plus approximately 1,000 µF centrally. Retain local 100 nF ceramic bypassing. These larger bulk values are engineering starting points, not Toshiba’s required values.**
- **[OBS] Modern MOSFET bridges provide intrinsic recirculation paths; DRV8871 explicitly specifies body-diode characteristics. L293D includes clamp diodes, whereas bare L298 requires external diodes.** [DRV8871 p.5](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/drv8871.pdf), [HTC L293D p.8](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/L293D.pdf), [ST L298 application circuits](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf)
- **[INF] These paths do not prevent supply-rail rise from regeneration. An AC adapter generally should not be assumed to absorb returned energy. Do not place one ordinary flyback diode directly across a bidirectionally driven coil.**
- **[INF] A candidate clamp for the regulated 12 V/TB67H450 version is P4KE15A across VM–GND after the fuse, cathode to VM.**
- **[OBS] P4KE15A has 12.8 V working standoff and 21.2 V maximum specified clamping voltage at its rated pulse current; its 400 W rating is a short-pulse rating.** [Datasheet](https://akizukidenshi.com/goodsaffix/P4KE.pdf)
- **[INF] This is compatible with 35 V capacitors and the TB67H450’s voltage margin, but must be checked against the actual adapter tolerance and measured regeneration. It is not a continuous braking load, nor a suitable automatically transferable clamp for an 11 V-limited driver.**
- **[INF] Starting fuse choices: approximately 3 A DC-rated main fuse and 0.75–1 A DC-rated branch fuses. Coordinate them with wire ratings, adapter fault behavior, and measured inrush. Fuses do not establish a safe coil temperature.**

### Communication loss

- **[INF] Implement the communication-loss cutoff at ≤400 ms from the last qualifying valid traffic, leaving margin below the required 0.5 s. Force every bridge OFF and discard queued note activations when it trips.**
- **[INF] Long notes with no intervening traffic cannot distinguish silence from a broken link. The existing protocol must provide a liveness basis; this is a dependency for its author to resolve.**
- **[INF] Add a hardware watchdog, for example around 250 ms, serviced only when the relevant firmware is healthy. PWM/PIO can continue while application code is stuck, so a normal-loop timeout alone does not cover that failure.**
- **[INF] If the 0.5 s requirement must survive broader firmware failures, use an external watchdog and a hardware gate that forces both bridge inputs LOW. Validate boot, reset, unplugged UART, stalled firmware, and power sequencing on the assembled hardware.**
